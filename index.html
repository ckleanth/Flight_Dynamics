Case study · flight dynamics · CFD

Static stability of rockets from Mach 0.1 to 2

A rocket flies straight when the normal force that a gust creates acts behind its centre of gravity. Four model rockets of one family share a 180 mm ogive nose, a 60 mm body, four large swept fins and an Estes D12-3 motor; their bodies are 400, 450, 631 and 850 mm long. The shortest was added to the family as the one expected to be unstable.

Sixteen steady RANS cases in OpenFOAM compute the 400 and 850 mm rockets at Mach 0.1, 0.8, 1.5 and 2 and at angles of attack of 0 and 4°. They are compared with Barrowman's method, OpenRocket's method and eight cases in Ansys CFX, and the wall forces are split by part to show why both rockets are stable at every Mach number.

The rockets

Four rockets, one family, one question

The study is a capabilities exercise: it predicts the centre of pressure (CP), the normal-force slope and the static margin of model rockets across Mach numbers with hand methods and with CFD, and turns the CFD into pictures and videos. Barrowman's method gives the CP of the four rockets at low speed, and OpenRocket's method its change with Mach number. OpenFOAM then computes the shortest and the longest rocket at four Mach numbers.

The fins are large for the body: their tips lie 240 mm apart, four body diameters, and their leading edge is swept so far that the tips end 70 mm behind the base. On its D12-3 motor each rocket reaches about Mach 0.11 to 0.15 in a vertical flight (an estimate with an ASSUMED drag coefficient); the cases at Mach 0.8, 1.5 and 2 take the same shapes through the transonic and supersonic range.

Stability in one number

The forces on the rocket are reduced to coefficients of the whole rocket in body axes: the axial force Fx along the axis, the normal force Fz in the plane of the angle of attack and the pitching moment My about a reference point on the axis, with the free stream's dynamic pressure q∞:

CA = Fx / (q∞ Aref)CN = Fz / (q∞ Aref)Cm = My / (q∞ Aref d)

The CP is the point on the axis about which the normal force has no moment, and the static margin SM is the CP's distance behind the CG, in calibres:

xCP = xref − d Cm / CNSM = (xCP − xCG) / d

A positive margin makes the rocket statically stable: when a gust raises the nose, the normal force acts behind the CG, and its moment turns the nose back into the wind. Barrowman's rule asks for at least one calibre. By Barrowman's method the four rockets have 0.49, 0.70, 1.53 and 2.64 calibres at launch, so the 400 mm rocket is the marginal one: whether it is stable at every Mach number is the question the CFD answers.

The flow

From Mach 0.1 to 2 in videos and pictures

At Mach 0.1 the flow speeds up over the nose shoulder and leaves a slow wake behind the base and the fins. At Mach 0.8 the flow over the nose shoulder and the fins approaches sonic speed but stays subsonic. At Mach 1.5 and 2 shocks spring from the nose tip and the fin leading edges, the flow expands round the base and recompresses behind it, and the shocks lean back more steeply at Mach 2.

Results

Stable at every Mach number

Both rockets are statically stable at all four Mach numbers, at launch and at burnout. At 4° the 400 mm rocket has 0.60 to 0.79 calibre at launch, below Barrowman's rule of one calibre, and the 850 mm rocket 2.43 to 3.13. The margins grow at burnout, as the CG moves forward with the burnt propellant.

The normal force at 4° rises from Mach 0.1 to a peak at Mach 0.8 and falls above it; the slope CNα runs from 17.9 to 28.0 per radian. OpenFOAM gives 1.31 to 1.86 times the normal force of OpenRocket's method, the most at Mach 2, where that method's supersonic fins lose lift fastest, and places the CP 7.0 to 71.0 mm further aft. Its margins therefore exceed those of OpenRocket's method at every Mach number, by 0.12 to 1.18 calibre: at Mach 2 that method leaves the 400 mm rocket 0.17 calibre, OpenFOAM 0.63.

Why they are stable

The fins win the balance of moments

The wall of each case is split into the nose, the cylindrical body, the fins and the base, and the wall forces are summed over each part, so the normal force of each part and the point where it acts can be read. On both rockets and at every Mach number the fins carry 74 to 78 % of the normal force and the nose 7 to 14 %; the body carries the rest.

On the 400 mm rocket the fins' normal force acts 374 to 389 mm from the nose tip, 68 to 83 mm behind the launch CG, and the nose's 82 to 96 mm from the tip, 210 to 224 mm ahead of it. The body's normal force acts beside the fin roots, which begin at 292 mm, where the fins' pressure field reaches the body; Barrowman's method books this part of the lift to the fins. It too acts behind the CG.

The moments about the CG

Each part's moment about the launch CG, divided by the whole normal force, is that part's share of the static margin, and the shares add up to the margin:

SMi = −Cm,CG,i / CNΣi SMi = SM

On the 400 mm rocket the fins add 0.86 to 1.07 calibre and the nose takes 0.27 to 0.46: the fins' restoring moment is 2.3 to 3.5 times the nose's upsetting moment. The body adds 0.03 to 0.08 calibre. On the 850 mm rocket the fins add 3.10 to 3.41 calibres against 0.58 to 1.07 from the nose, and the body 0.18 to 0.49.

The fins

Large and far aft

Their four planforms add up to 27,180 mm², 9.6 times the body's cross-section, and Barrowman's method gives them 7.3 times the normal-force slope of the nose. Their sweep carries their area to the base and beyond: on the 400 mm rocket their normal force acts 11 to 26 mm ahead of the base. A gust that raises the nose creates a force on the fins 5.7 to 10.7 times the force on the nose, on a lever arm about the CG 31 to 39 % as long as the nose's.

The nose and the Mach number

Above Mach 1 the nose gains, the fins lose

At Mach 0.1 the nose's normal force agrees with slender-body theory: 0.140 at 88 mm, against 0.140 at 84 mm in Barrowman's method. Above Mach 1 it rises to 0.170 and moves aft to 96 mm, while the fins' falls from 1.492 at Mach 0.8 to 0.974 at Mach 2: the 400 mm rocket's margin falls from 0.79 at Mach 1.5 to 0.63 at Mach 2. The fins' normal force moves aft at the same time, from 374 to 389 mm, which keeps the margin above its low-speed value.

The mass

The heavy parts sit at the tail

The CG of the 400 mm rocket lies 77 % of the way from the nose tip: the fins, the motor tube and the motor with its propellant weigh 130.6 g together, 55 % of the launch mass. The fins alone weigh 67.4 g, 28 % of the rocket, and pull the CG towards the CP they create; yet they move the CP 252 mm aft (Barrowman's method) and the CG only 33 mm.

The length

The CG follows the CP

Lengthening the body moves the fins, and with them the CP, aft by the added length, but moves the CG less, because the nose and the recovery parts stay in front. From 400 to 450 mm, Barrowman's CP moves 44.0 mm aft and the CG 31.2 mm, so 50 mm of body add 0.21 calibre. From 400 to 850 mm at Mach 0.1, OpenFOAM's CP moves 406.4 mm and the CG 267.1 mm, and the margin grows from 0.60 to 2.92 calibres. Shortening works the other way, but slowly: the CG follows the CP, and the 400 mm rocket, the shortest of the family, keeps 0.49 calibre in Barrowman's method and 0.60 to 0.79 in OpenFOAM.

Neutral stability

Marginal, not unstable

Neutral stability needs the CG at the CP: for the 400 mm rocket at Mach 0.1 and 4°, 35.9 mm aft of the launch CG, at 342.2 mm. With the mass model that takes 39.5 g off the 41.4 g nose, or 179 g more at the fins, 2.7 times their own mass: both far outside a printed rocket of this design. A built rocket's margin follows from its own CG, which this study does not measure; for any plausible build of this design the 400 mm rocket is stable with a margin below one calibre: marginal, not unstable.

At burnout

More stable as the motor burns

As the 21.1 g of propellant burn at the tail, the CG moves forward by 5.7 mm in the 400 mm rocket and 17.4 mm in the 850 mm rocket, and the margins grow by 0.10 and 0.29 calibre.

Why the CFD margins exceed the hand methods'At Mach 0.1 and 4° the CFD's nose matches Barrowman's. The difference sits at the tail: OpenFOAM puts 1.313 on the fins and 0.275 on the body beside them, 1.588 together, against 1.025 for Barrowman's fins with their body interference and 0.039 for the body's crossflow lift, which Barrowman's method places ahead of the CG. More normal force behind the CG moves the CP aft, from Barrowman's 332.0 mm to OpenFOAM's 342.2 mm. At Mach 2 OpenRocket's method lets the supersonic fins lose lift faster than the CFD shows: CN = 0.687 with the CP at 316.7 mm, against 1.276 and 343.9 mm from OpenFOAM. The hand methods are therefore on the safe side for these rockets: they predict smaller margins than the CFD.

Two CFD codes and the hand methods

CFX and OpenFOAM against Barrowman's method

Within the time of this study, Ansys CFX ran 8 of its 57 planned cases: the 400 and 450 mm rockets at Mach 0.1 and 0, 2, 4 and 8°. At Mach 0.1 and 4° on the 400 mm rocket, OpenFOAM's normal force is 9 % above CFX's and its CP 2.8 mm further aft. In every case at incidence, CFX gives more normal force than Barrowman's method and a CP further aft, as OpenFOAM does: both CFD codes put the rockets' margins above the hand method's.

The model

Half models, four meshes, two solver set-ups

The half rocket, with the symmetry plane through its axis, sits in a cylinder that reaches 5 overall lengths upstream and 15 downstream of the rocket, with a radius of 10 overall lengths. Ansys Meshing fills it with tetrahedra and grows 35 prism layers on every wall, from a first layer of 2 µm at a growth ratio of 1.2; the surface cells are 1 mm on the fins and 2.5 mm elsewhere. The supersonic cases use meshes with a refinement zone for the shocks: a cylinder of 4 body diameters in radius, from 0.1 overall lengths ahead of the nose tip to 1.5 behind the fin tips, filled with 10 mm cells. CFX uses the same meshes.

Two solver set-ups

At Mach 0.1, rhoSimpleFoam solves the steady equations with SIMPLE (set-up E0); at Mach 0.8, 1.5 and 2, rhoPimpleFoam marches in local pseudo-time steps to the steady state (set-up L0). Both start with first-order upwind convection and then switch to second-order linear-upwind convection with limited gradients, and both keep the pressure between set limits during the start.

Air is a perfect gas of molar mass 28.96 kg/kmol and constant specific heat, with Sutherland's viscosity and a Prandtl number of 0.690. Turbulence follows the k-ω SST model with wall functions that hold at any y+. The free stream is the sea-level standard atmosphere, 288.15 K and 101325 Pa, with an ASSUMED turbulence intensity of 1 % and eddy-viscosity ratio of 10.

Runs

One case per cloud container

Each case ran alone on 4 cores of an Intel Xeon processor, in parts of 500 iterations: at least 1000 iterations at Mach 0.1 and 0.8 and 1500 at Mach 1.5 and 2, at most 4000 and 5000. A run stops once CA, CN and Cm each stay within 0.1 % of their mean over the last 200 iterations (with floors on the mean for coefficients near zero), or at its cap.

Health gate

Checked after the start-up

After the start-up a gate checks that the run is free of fatal errors, that its residuals have fallen, that the pressure no longer touches its limits and that the axial force no longer alternates. A last check confirms that each case ended cleanly and that its force coefficients equal the doubled forces of the half model.

CFX

The same meshes and air

CFX ran the 400 and 450 mm rockets at Mach 0.1, steady, with the SST model and automatic wall functions, total energy with the viscous work and High Resolution advection. A case stopped at 500 iterations or by the OpenFOAM rule; each angle after the first started from the previous angle's result.

Hand methods

Barrowman and OpenRocket

Barrowman's method adds the normal-force slopes of the nose and the fins and places each at its own CP; at an angle of attack the body's crossflow lift is added. OpenRocket's method adds the Mach number: compressibility corrections of the fins below Mach 1, Busemann's supersonic theory above it, and a fin CP that moves aft as the Mach number rises.

Force split

Forces by part from the wall

The wall pressure and the axial part of the wall shear are summed over the nose (to 180 mm from the tip), the body, the fins and the base. The pressure force and its moment reproduce the solver's own to a relative 10−4; the small viscous normal force comes from the solver's force file.

Barrowman's method in brief

For an ogive nose of length Ln, and N fins of semi-span s, root chord cr, tip chord ct and mid-chord line lm on a body of radius R:

(CNα)n = 2xn = 0.466 Ln

(CNα)f =Kfb · 4N (s/d)2 / [1 + √(1 + (2lm / (cr + ct))2)]Kfb = 1 + R / (s + R)

xCP = Σ (CNα)i xi / Σ (CNα)i

For the 400 mm rocket

The nose gives 2.0 per radian at 83.9 mm and the fins 14.68 per radian, with Kfb = 1.25, at 369.9 mm, so the CP lies at 335.6 mm: 29.3 mm behind the launch CG, 0.49 calibre. At 4° the body's crossflow lift adds 0.039 to CN at 225.8 mm, ahead of the CG.

Verification

Checked before the results were trusted

Six cases met the convergence rule; the other 10 ran to their caps. At 0° the normal force and the pitching moment are noise around zero and never met their absolute bands, and at Mach 0.8 the forces wander slowly over several hundred iterations. Over the last 20 % of the Mach 0.8 runs, the mean of CA lies 0.029 to 0.041 above its mean over the last 200 iterations, while at 4° CN differs by at most 1.3 % and xCP by at most 1.4 mm: the stability results do not depend on the window. Every case passed its final checks.

Each case took from 1.8 to 32.4 h of wall time on its 4 cores, 269 h in all (1,078 core-hours); the containers ran side by side.

Problems found and fixed

Fourteen problems, each with its evidence and fix

The study's report ends with a log of the problems met while the meshes were made, the cases run and the results post-processed. Each had its evidence, its fix and a result that shows the fix held.

Starting the solvers
  • An impulsive start of rhoSimpleFoam from the free stream diverged in every container that reported, with floating-point exceptions at iterations 36 to 95. At Mach 0.1 the SIMPLE set-up E0, with strong under-relaxation, pressure and velocity limits and first-order schemes to iteration 200, passed its gate at iteration 500; SIMPLE is not used at Mach 0.8.
  • Local time stepping with the default smoothing froze the flow near the body: the pseudo-time step was tied to the 5.9 × 10−10 s of the wall cells, and 621 of 800 pressure solves did no iteration. With set-up L0 (a smoothing coefficient of 0.5, at least one iteration in every pressure solve and the transonic formulation) the Mach 0.8 pilot passed its gate at iteration 800 with no warning.
  • At a Courant number of 0.5 the pressure force alternated from one pseudo-time step to the next, by up to 0.13 in CA. With 0.1 in the start-up, 0.2 after it and a gate test on the swing, the swing fell to 7.6 × 10−6 against a band of 6.87 × 10−3.
Sixteen cases at once
  • Static checks alone missed a fault that stopped every case at iteration 0 on the symmetry patch, and the first gate could not tell a frozen field from a healthy one. One update of 872,032 bytes brought the two set-ups, a health gate, a start-up test, a divergence guard and nine script fixes; a pilot applied it first, and all 16 cases ended with their acceptance checks passed.
  • At Mach 2 the base flow failed the gate at iteration 800 while the base filled: the pressure sat on its lower limit and the temperature reached 704 to 782 K against a bound of 700 K. A gate that fails only on those limits is judged again every 100 iterations; the four Mach 2 cases passed at iterations 1000 to 1100.
  • The cloud machines restarted several times a night. Each case resumed from its latest written time; the largest loss took about 30 minutes.
  • Each case returns one zip with its SHA-256: 16 zips, 994,070,819 bytes, all passing their byte count, SHA-256 and CRC checks, and the analysis reads them in place.
Meshes and convergence
  • OpenFOAM v1912's mesh converter reads only the text sections of a Fluent mesh, and the exported meshes carry their data in binary sections. A transcoder wrote the same meshes as text, checked node for node and face for face; the 4.14 million cells of the 400 mm mesh were prepared in 2 min 1 s.
  • checkMesh found non-orthogonality up to 87.9° (23.3° on average) with 14,186 faces above 70°, as expected for 2 µm wall layers. The meshes were kept, as CFX uses the same ones, with limited corrected schemes and two non-orthogonal correctors.
  • Ten of the sixteen cases ended at their iteration caps. The results give the means over the last 200 iterations with the ranges over the last 20 % of each run, and the CP only at incidence.
CFX
  • CFX refuses a specified flow direction within 10 degrees of a boundary's tangent, and the free stream runs almost along the far-field cylinder. The far-field opening takes its direction from a linearisation by the velocity magnitude; the tests and all eight production cases ran.
  • The CFX chain stopped when its window was closed, 304 iterations into its ninth case. At this stage it was not restarted: the comparisons use the OpenFOAM results, and CFX ran 8 of its 57 planned cases.
Pictures and videos
  • ParaView 6.1's pvbatch wrote black pictures after about 20 views in one process: one process per case and a check of every picture for blank output; all 40 pictures pass.
  • A schlieren scaled by its largest gradient, at the nose tip 10 to 15 times the 90th percentile, was nearly white: it is scaled by the 90th percentile.
  • The video exports counted .vtk files while v1912 writes .vtp, and left off-value points at the rims of the iso-surfaces: ParaView trims the rim cells before rendering and counts them, and the Mach 2 orbit keeps 233,463 of 233,463 compression cells and 415,282 of 415,347 expansion cells.
The force split
  • v1912's VTK writer gives four times the true byte count in the connectivity header: the reader checks every array against the counts of its piece.
  • The written wall shear gives a viscous normal force of −0.0149 N against +0.0067 N in the force file (400 mm rocket, Mach 0.1, 4°, half model): the split takes the viscous normal force from the force file, and reproduces the solver's forces to 1 × 10−4.

Status

The first phase: OpenFOAM, with CFX in part

Limits

  • The margins rest on an ASSUMED mass model; a measured CG moves every margin by one calibre for each 60 mm of difference.
  • The cases are steady RANS solutions. At Mach 0.8 the forces wander over several hundred iterations; 10 of the 16 cases ended at their caps, with their spreads over the last 20 % of each run given.
  • Each rocket was computed at 0 and 4° only: the normal-force slope is a secant, and the behaviour at larger angles is not computed.
  • The half models hold symmetric flow in the pitch plane, with the fins in one roll position: a pair of fins in the plane of the angle of attack.
  • The largest y+ on the walls reaches 4.98 at Mach 2, on the fins; the wall functions hold at any y+.
  • CFX ran 8 of its 57 planned cases, all at Mach 0.1.
